# FootballSim

An original, offense-only 3D American football practice game for GitHub Pages. You run reps on an outdoor practice field: call a play, read a CPU defense from a low camera behind the offense, play quarterback, and take over the receiver or running back once he has the ball.

## Play and publish

Intended Pages address: **https://soldoutbudokan.github.io/FootballSim/**.

One-time repository setup: open **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source → GitHub Actions**. The `Verify FootballSim` workflow runs the simulation and graphics tests, the module and asset checks, the release build and a Chromium gameplay test. A successful push verification on `main` triggers `Publish GitHub Pages`, which can also be run by hand from Actions. No account or backend is needed to play.

The release contains every runtime file: Three.js 0.180.0 and its addons, textures, the HDR environment and the interface fonts. All of them are downloaded from pinned commits or tags and hash-verified by `scripts/vendor.mjs`; credits and licences are in `ASSETS.md`. The published game makes no third-party requests. Paths are relative, so it works under `/FootballSim/` or any other static subdirectory, and the build versions CSS and the whole JavaScript import graph together so a browser never mixes two releases.

## The practice facility

- **Two natural-grass NFL fields** side by side, 120 × 53⅓ yd, with 4-inch lines, 8-inch goal lines, a 6 ft white border, hash marks at the NFL inbound lines (70 ft 9 in from each sideline), sideline ticks, numbers every 10 yards 12 yards in from the sideline with direction arrows, yellow gooseneck goal posts and orange pylons. No midfield logo and no end-zone lettering. The turf is mown natural grass: the 5-yard mowing bands lean in alternating directions (cross-cut in the end zones), so they are light or dark, and more or less glossy, depending on where the camera and the sun are, and they swap as the camera moves around the field. Yard-scale clumps of colour, worn grass between the hashes, blade-scale normals up close and paint that is sprayed on the grass (ragged at the edges, green in the troughs, thin where it is worn) keep it from reading as a flat green plane.
- **Around the field:** a two-storey team building with a glass front and a deep roof overhang behind the offense, a green windscreen fence on chain-link posts, four practice light poles, a scaffold film tower, a scissor lift with a camera platform, a small aluminium bleacher at the 50, and sideline equipment (two-man sled, cones, ball cart, water station, trainer's tent, tackling dummies, benches, a play clock on a trailer) with a few staff figures. Beyond the fence are trees, gentle terrain, a parking lot and a few low buildings. There is no stadium, crowd, skyline or scoreboard.
- **Sky and light:** the three.js physically based `Sky` with procedural clouds drives the sun and the fog colour. *Afternoon* uses an HDR environment for reflections; *Golden hour* has a low warm sun and long shadows; *Under the lights* uses four pole-light banks with visible pools, the nearest one casting shadows. The clouds shade themselves away from the sun. Haze is analytic height fog (`env/atmosphere.js`): densest at the ground and thinning upward, so the far end zone, the tree belts and the hills recede in layers and meet the sky's horizon colour.
- **Shadows:** a tight shadow frustum (about 48 × 48 yd) follows the ball and the controlled player each frame; every player also has a soft contact shadow. Instanced grass tufts with a cheap wind sway grow around the camera on the low views.
- **The image:** from Balanced up the scene is rendered in HDR and finished by a small post-processing pipeline written for this game (`env/post.js`): ambient occlusion from depth (High and Ultra), bloom of what is brighter than paper white only (helmet glints, lamp lenses, the low sun), ACES filmic tone mapping with an exposure per lighting preset, a restrained grade per preset (a little contrast, slightly reduced saturation, cool shadows and warm highlights), a light vignette and fine grain that also removes banding. The title screen and the replay add depth of field behind the players. The aim is a broadcast camera pointed at a real field, not a filter; `tests/graphics.test.mjs` bounds the grade.
- **Trees:** broadleaf crowns are overlapping lobes of leaf-clump cards with rounded normals, darker inside the crown, and glow when the sun is behind them; spruces are crossed painted cards.

## Athletes

Twenty-six procedural athletes (the offense and a defensive squad of fifteen, eleven of whom take the field in each personnel package) built around a 6 ft 2 in frame, with wider lineman builds and distinct skill-position proportions. Nothing is loaded: every form is generated at start-up.

- **Bodies.** Muscle and pad forms are sculpted into the meshes rather than left to shading: glutes, quadriceps and hamstrings, calves over slim ankles, biceps, triceps and a tapering forearm, a neck with the slope of the trapezius; the jersey drapes over a wide shoulder-pad yoke with a cap over each shoulder, its plate edge showing front and back, and bunches where it is tucked into the belt. Occlusion is baked into the vertex colours (under the arms and the pads, at the belt, behind the knee pads, inside the helmet).
- **Equipment.** A modern helmet shell, longer than it is wide, with a raised centre spine, a brow bumper, cheek flaps down to the jaw, ear holes, vents and a rear bumper; an open facemask for the skill positions and a closed cage for linemen and linebackers, both clipped to the cheek flaps; a chin cup on four straps and a tinted visor on some. Pants carry thigh and knee pads and a belt; cleats have a moulded upper, a padded collar, an outsole and seven studs; gloves have four fingers, a thumb and a grip palm. Tape, compression sleeves and towels vary by player.
- **Materials.** Physically based and distinct per surface, in one draw call: roughness, metalness and sheen are stored per vertex, and each part picks a layer of a small tiling detail array (perforated jersey mesh, stretch knit, skin, rib knit, pebbled grain, plain weave) that bends its normal and varies its roughness. Jerseys and pants have a cloth sheen, skin is matte, helmets are clear-coated, the ball is pebbled leather with a normal map, seams, stripes and raised laces. The Performance tier compiles the body shader without the detail layers and the sheen.
- **Numbers and trim.** Athletic block numerals with a twill outline on the chest, the back and both shoulder caps; two sleeve stripes; a navy-white-navy stripe down the pants; hooped socks over white; a silver helmet stripe and the club's chevrons on both sides of the shell. One 2048 px atlas holds every jersey (torso and sleeves per player) and the shared pants and socks.

Each rig is driven by inverse kinematics and skinned on the GPU, one merged mesh per material for the whole squad, with a reduced level of detail for distant players. An athlete is about 10,000 triangles at full detail and 3,000 reduced. The squad is built once; a defender who is off the field this rep is simply not drawn.

The team is a fictional navy-and-silver club: navy helmets with a silver stripe and silver chevrons, silver pants, the offense in navy practice jerseys, the defense in white, and the quarterback in the red non-contact jersey. It carries no real league or team marks.

The simulation drives a procedural animation; every pose is worked out from the snapshot and the clock, so a replay shows the same motion at any speed and in either direction.

- **Running.** Stride length and cadence follow speed, and the stride eases through the sudden speed changes of a hit. The body leans with acceleration, sits back and reaches its feet out to brake, and banks into turns; the plant foot of a cut or juke steps to its spot and holds there. Each position runs its own way (linemen wide and flat-footed, receivers and corners high-kneed, backs low) and each player varies it a little, so no two men stride alike. Defensive backs backpedal and flip their hips. Linemen and backs set in their stances.
- **Tackles.** A tackle is one event shared by everyone in it. The snapshot carries who hit whom, when, and both men's velocity going in; from that the athletes work out which way the pile goes down and how hard. A free defender closing on the ball breaks down first: hips sunk, base wide, arms cocked, eyes on the runner's hips; the runner drops his pads and puts both arms on the ball. At contact the tackler shoots low with his head to one side and locks his arms around the runner's legs; the runner's hips go with the hit while his shoulders and head lag. He then goes down on his front (dragged down from behind, or falling forward), on his back (stopped by a man coming the other way) or on his side (hit from the side), turning to lie along the fall. The tackler rides him down onto his legs; a man in front who is dragged along goes down on his hip beside him; a diver goes out flat at the shins; a tackler the runner pulls away from is stretched out and dragged. A second man in hits high and stays over the pile on a knee. A hard hit puts the runner down in about half a second, a drag from behind takes most of a second. After the whistle the tackler, then the runner, push up off the turf and stand; the recording keeps two seconds past the whistle so the replay shows it.
- **Missed tackles.** The man who misses closes his arms on air and stumbles toward the side the runner went by, a hand down to the turf (lower still if he had left his feet). The runner lurches, stiff-arms a man on his free side or turns his shoulders away from one on the ball side, and gets his feet back under him.
- **Throwing.** The quarterback drops, sets and throws through a full motion: his hips load and fire first, the shoulders stay closed a beat longer, the front arm points and tucks, the elbow leads the hand through and the back foot drags; the ball leaves his hand 0.23 s into the 0.4 s throw. On a run he rides the ball out to the back's belt, and the back makes a pocket for it.
- **Catching.** A receiver reaches for the ball with both hands where it will arrive, but only if it passes within about 1.3 yd of him at a catchable height. He goes up for a high ball, sinks for a low one, turns his shoulders to one thrown across him, and at speed looks one in over his shoulder without turning his hips from his run; then he brings it in to the tuck. A receiver who does not come down with it lets his hands go after the ball and drops his eyes to it; a defender swats through it with the near arm.
- **Blocks.** A blocker punches, then the two men hand-fight (each arm presses and gives in its own rhythm, the bodies rocking) and a pass rusher now and then throws an arm over. A beaten blocker stumbles; a defender pancaked on a run goes down on his back and bounces up. A man who gets away is let go of.
- **Between plays.** After the whistle players stand with their weight on one leg and watch the ball, most with their hands on their hips. Defenders near an incompletion wave it off; a scorer raises an arm and teammates near him put both arms up.

After the snap, pass blockers sit in a pass set and kick-slide. Run blockers and rushers stay low with their hands up; on a run the line never shows a pass set. Coverage players hold a ready crouch. The quarterback sets in the pocket on staggered feet with the ball at his chest. An engaged blocker and rusher lean only as far as the gap between them allows, hands on the other man's pads and each helmet to its own side, so the two never pass through each other. A replay that opens on the snap starts from the stances. The practice ball is a prolate spheroid with laces and stripes; it spirals nose-first along its flight.

Player models: drop a rigged character at dist/models/player.glb (and optional clips at dist/models/clips/<slot>.glb|.fbx) to replace the procedural athletes. See MODELS.md. ?athletes=procedural|skinned|dev overrides the choice.

## Movement and play

The simulation runs at a fixed 120 Hz, in yards and seconds, on a seeded random number generator.

- **Momentum.** Every player has a top speed, an acceleration curve, braking and lateral grip for his position (receivers and corners about 9.3 yd/s, backs and safeties 9.0, linebackers and tight ends about 8.3, the quarterback 7.6, linemen about 7). Acceleration tapers toward top speed, sideways force is limited by grip, so a fast runner turns on a wide arc and a sharp cut needs him to slow down first. A sprinting back needs about 0.6 s to stop and drifts 2–3 yd forward before he can reverse.
- **Cuts and jukes.** A change of direction over about 55° at speed is an automatic plant-and-cut: the runner brakes in proportion to the angle, plants and drives out. Q/E jukes are hard lateral plants that need some speed; they chain every 0.45 s and each one costs speed and a little stamina. There is no stun: defenders are beaten by their own momentum and reaction time.
- **Routes.** Receivers run their stems at full speed and slow into each break by its angle: the route asks for 55 % of top speed at a 90° out and 35 % at a comeback, then the runner plants and drives out. Each receiver reads the defense as it was 0.25 s ago, at every difficulty, so difficulty stays a property of the defense.
  - *Man or zone.* He scores how closely the nearest coverage defender stays with him: within 4.5 yd (in full within 2.5 yd), less how fast the gap between them opens or closes. The score settles over 0.35 s, and above 0.45 he reads man. A man trailing him scores near 1; a corner squatting in the flat or a safety bailing away scores near 0.
  - *Sit or run.* A curl, hitch, stick, snag or checkdown decides at its break. He keeps running if he reads man, if a defender will be within 2.5 yd of his spot when he gets there, or if one is driving on it at over 3 yd/s from within 6 yd: he breaks 8 yd across, away from his man's leverage, and 1.5 yd back toward the line. Otherwise he sits in the hole facing the quarterback, and slides 1.5 yd away from a defender who comes within 2 yd (at most every 0.6 s, and no more than 2.5 yd off his landmark).
  - *Crossers.* Against zone a crosser settles in the first hole on his way: after 4 yd of his crossing leg, shallower than 18 yd, with 3.5 yd to every defender and none closing on the spot faster than 3 yd/s. A defender within 2 yd gets him moving again for good. Against man he never sits.
  - *Seams and posts.* At 9 yd a seam bends to the middle of the gap between the deep defenders on either side of him: away from a single-high safety, between two-high safeties. He bends at most 3.5 yd and stays 2.5 yd on his own side of the ball. A post against one deep defender in the middle narrows to a skinny post, 45 % as wide; against two-high or none it stays a true post.
  - *Late in the down.* After 3 s a vertical deeper than 12 yd with a defender within 2 yd of him works 5 yd back toward the quarterback at 70 % speed. A crosser, slant or vertical that runs out of landmarks keeps going along his last leg, to 23.5 yd from the middle of the field or 55 yd past the line.
  - *Scramble drill.* When the quarterback leaves the pocket off script (rolling to the called side on a boot is the script), the receivers see it 0.25 s later and break to his side, in lanes 5 yd apart in the order they already stand. Those shallower than 10 yd work toward his sideline, 3–7 yd past the line and at least 5 yd past him. The deeper ones go deep on that side, at least 8 yd past him, at least 4 yd deeper than they were and 16 yd or more past the line. Nobody goes wider than 24 yd from the middle. Once he is over the line they stop running routes and block for him (see *Free blockers* under Run blocking).
  - *Release.* A press corner's jam damps the receiver's run to 30 % and pushes him across at 1.6 yd/s for the length of the jam (0.3–0.5 s, by the call). He wins a clean release 40 % of the time, and then the jam lasts 40 % as long. Otherwise he is held 0.3–0.6 s longer, and his next landmark, if shallower than 16 yd, moves 0.8–2 yd the way he was pushed.
- **The quarterback.** Untouched, he runs his drop; the stick always wins. From the gun he takes three steps back to 7 yd at 62 % of his speed, or settles at 6 yd on the quick game; under centre he takes a five-step drop to 7 yd at 80 %, opened to his throwing side. Once he has been steered, letting go of the stick sets his feet square to the line.
  - *Play action.* He meets the back where the back's first leg passes nearest him, 0.9 yd to the side away from where the back goes next, and rides the fake at half speed until the back has come within 1.8 yd and moved 0.4 yd away again (0.6–0.9 s after the snap). He cannot throw before then, and sets at 7.5 yd. On the boot he rolls away from the fake at 85 % speed to 11.5 yd outside the ball and 5 yd behind the line, his shoulders turned 40° from downfield toward his run.
  - *Accuracy.* He aims where a copy of the receiver, running the same route logic through the same movement model, will be when the ball arrives, so a throw meets him in stride. A set throw lands within half its spread of that point, long or short and to either side. The spread is 0.18 yd plus 0.035 yd (bullet), 0.045 yd (touch) or 0.06 yd (lob) for each yard past 5 yd: about 1.4, 1.8 and 2.3 yd on a 40 yd throw. Every 7 yd/s of movement adds 0.6 yd running at the target, 1.6 yd across the throw and 2.8 yd throwing back across his body; his speed counts as at least 60 % of what it was 0.2 s ago, so a quarterback who has just stopped is not yet set. A defender within 3.3 yd adds up to 2.8 yd as he closes to 0.6 yd.
  - *Placement.* From 15 yd (in full from 25 yd) he puts the ball where only his man can get it. He judges each defender at the catch: carried on at his own speed for up to 1 s, and able to close 35 % of his top speed faster once he reads the ball, 0.5 s after the release. One who can get within 2 yd of the catch (in full within 0.6 yd) pushes it up to 1 yd away from him, square to the throw or back toward the quarterback (the back shoulder), never beyond the receiver. The ball comes in 0.45 yd off the receiver's body on that side, and the miss leans the same way. A lob comes in 1.8 yd high, over a trailing man; a bullet at 1.6 yd.
  - *Leading.* The arrows held at the release move the ball off that spot. Up and down go along the receiver's run as he gets to the ball: up in front of him, so he runs onto it away from a man trailing him; down behind him, back toward the quarterback (the back shoulder). Left and right go toward that sideline as seen from behind the offense. A receiver slower than 2 yd/s has no run to lead, and up is away from the quarterback. The ball goes as far that way as he can still reach: a copy of him runs his route for 0.12 s (he sees the ball out), then runs to the spot to get there with the ball, and must end within 0.6 yd of the ball as it comes in (or no farther off than without the lead). It goes 80 % of that room, at most 3 yd (2 yd behind him) and never within 1 yd of the sideline or the end line: about 0.4 yd in front of a quick slant, 0.7 yd in front of a go route and up to 2.4 yd to the side of one, 1.6 yd behind either. The placement still moves the ball away from a defender, but never back against the lead.
  - *Contested catches.* Every catch has a 3 % drop chance, 11 % for a bullet from inside 8 yd. A defender within 1.8 yd adds up to 50 % when he is between the receiver and the ball and 30 % when he trails (in full within 0.6 yd); that part ends as a breakup at the catch, or, when he has seen the ball and faces it, as an interception: half his hands odds (corner 52 %, safety 56 %, linebacker 40 %) when he is between the receiver and the ball, a quarter of that when he trails. A ball forced into a tight window is picked.
- **Play calling.** On **Coordinator's call** the offensive coordinator (`dist/js/sim/offense.js`) calls each rep from the down, the distance and the field, with one random draw, so a seed makes the same calls. He profiles every play from its routes: a run, play action, the quick game, an intermediate dropback, or a shot (one of the first two reads in the cue breaks 10 yd or deeper and runs past 25 yd), where its first reads break and how many routes can be caught at the sticks. 1st down is balanced, about 42 % runs; 2nd and 7 or more leans pass; 2 yd or less to go brings runs (inside runs first), the quick game and play action; 3rd and 3–6 calls intermediate routes that break past the sticks; 3rd and 4th and 7 or more never calls a designed run or a concept with no route that can be caught past the sticks, and from 15 yd mostly shots. Inside the 5 he runs, throws quick and fakes, with almost no shots; in the red zone a route that breaks beyond the end zone is wasted and reads should break near the goal line; backed up inside his own 10 he cuts the fakes and shots. Every play he can use keeps at least 40 % of an even share (less where it fits poorly), so the calls stay varied. **Repeat the spot** reps are a 1st and 10 to him unless the spot is in the red zone.
- **Defense.** A coordinator calls every rep: the personnel package, a coverage family and its variant, the shell it shows before the snap, the front, press or off, and man leverage. Each defender then plays what he saw a moment ago: a ball carrier, or a quarterback he rushes, 0.40 s late at Rookie, 0.28 s at Pro and 0.18 s at All-pro. Difficulty changes reads, anticipation and the coordinator's craft, never bodies, reach or catch odds (see [Difficulty](#difficulty)).
  - *Personnel.* Five packages from a squad of fifteen: base 4-3; nickel 4-2-5 (a nickel back, #21, for the Sam); dime 4-1-6 (a dime back, #29, for the Will too); heavy 4-4-3 (a fourth linebacker, #50, for the strong safety: eight in the box, one deep); and a 5-3-3 goal line (a nose tackle, #93, for the free safety). A receiver is detached when a wide receiver stands 6.8 yd or more from the ball, a tight end or back 9 yd or more (a tight end in a bunch stays a tight end). Two or fewer detached draw the base, three the nickel, four or five the dime; 3rd and long moves up one (base to nickel, nickel to dime); short yardage (2 yd or less) plays heavy against the tight sets, base against three wide and nickel against four or more; inside the 5 the goal line comes on, unless four or more are detached (then the nickel, with no Tampa 2 or quarters). The coordinator matches the offense on a quarter of calls at Rookie, two thirds at Pro and every call at All-pro, and otherwise stays in base, so a Rookie defense is often caught in a 4-3 against empty. Against a running offense he keeps the base on the field against three wide on up to 35 % of calls; against a passing one he brings the nickel against the base sets on up to 30 %. Everybody who comes on plays the jobs of the man he replaces with his own technique: the nickel and dime backs cover the slots like corners, line up over #2 and blitz in the pressure calls; the fourth linebacker plays the strong safety's box jobs from the box. Heavy plays only the one-high and pressure families (Cover 3, Cover 1, Cover 0, fire zone, sim pressure) from the one-high or mug look. At the goal line five linemen and the Sam fill all six gaps (A, B and C), the Mike and the Will stack 3 yd off behind the guards, the strong safety plays at the goal line (never deeper than 3 yd into the end zone) and the corners press; it plays Cover 1 (five men on five receivers, the safety over the top), Cover 0 (the man left over comes) and a Cover 3 compressed by the end line.
  - *Situation.* He reads down and distance, field position, the formation and a scouting report, never the play. A neutral down (1st and 10 between the 20s against two by two) leans to Cover 3, then Cover 2. Short yardage (2 yd or less) brings the box down and man and pressure up; 2nd and long (8 yd or more) expects a pass; 3rd and medium (3–6 yd) plays tight man and sim pressure; 3rd and long (7 yd or more) brings pressure and deep shells. Inside the 5 the deep zones give way to man and Cover 0 (and the goal-line package); in the red zone man and the three-deep zones rise while Tampa 2 and Cover 6 fade; an offense backed up inside its own 10 draws more Cover 0 and fire zones.
  - *Formation.* Quarters against two by two, Cover 6 and Cover 3 against trips, Cover 1 and quarters against a bunch, pressure and Cover 1 against empty, and a loaded box (Cover 3, Cover 1, Cover 0) against the tight sets.
  - *Tendencies.* The scouting report files what the offense does: runs and passes by formation, time to throw, scrambles, targets, deep completions and the calls it beats, each fading 3 % a rep. Against a running formation the coordinator loads the box, and his linebackers read runs sooner and bite harder on fakes. Against deep completions he plays two-high; against throws out in under 2.5 s he presses and plays man; against a quarterback who holds the ball past 2.9 s he brings pressure; against a scrambler he blitzes less and spies more. He calls less of what has been beaten (a first down, a touchdown, or 40 % of the distance on 1st down and 60 % on 2nd) and leans his help toward the favourite target.
  - *Menu.* A random call draws from 5 families at Rookie, 8 at Pro and all 10 at All-pro. No family gets less than 40 % of an even share, or more than 40 % of the calls, in any one situation. The Defense setting can call any family at any level.
  - *What it shows.* The call sets what the defense plays and, apart from it, what it shows: the family's own shell or, on about 10 % of calls at Rookie, 35 % at Pro and 50 % at All-pro, another one. Two-high puts the safeties at 12–13 yd, 9–10 yd either side of the ball. One-high puts the free safety at 14 yd, anywhere from 2.5 yd weak to 1 yd strong whatever he plays, and the strong safety at 8.5 yd by the box. Base puts both near 10 yd. The mug walks two men up into the A gaps or over the edge, whether they come or not, with the safeties at 9–10 yd and the corners pressed. Depth gives no rusher away: a linebacker who rushes from another look creeps to 3 yd and a safety to 7 yd, and on 6 %, 21 % or 30 % of calls (by level) a dropping linebacker creeps up too and, drawn apart, a strong safety with an underneath job comes down.
  - *Rotations.* A disguised call rotates at the snap, or holds the look first: 25 % of disguises at Pro for 0.3–0.45 s, 40 % at All-pro for 0.15–0.3 s, never at Rookie. Only the men the disguise moved hold it, and only for a trip of 4–10 yd. A man rotating more than 6 yd runs a crossover for 1.2 s, still facing the quarterback; a lineman who drops takes a rush step and leaves 0.25 s after his get-off.
  - *Physical limits.* A look the call cannot get out of in time is never shown. Cover 0 shows only the mug or base look; a safety who rushes never starts 12 yd deep, and from one-high only on the strong side; Cover 2 and Tampa 2 never show the mug; Cover 1 from two-high is always the robber call, with the strong safety coming down to rob. A pressure shown two-high has the farthest to rotate, so it shows that look 40 % as often.
  - *Alignment.* The strength is the side with more receivers, the backs aside (the tight end's side on a tie). The Sam linebacker and the strong safety play to it; the outside linebackers apex between the tackle and a wide #2; the post safety shades 3 yd toward trips; the Mike sits at 4.3 yd against four or more receivers within 9 yd of the ball and 5.2 yd otherwise. Man defenders are paired with receivers at the lowest total cost (corners on the #1s, a safety on a slot, a linebacker on a tight end or back) and travel with them through an audible; the family, shell and front stay. A corner over a #1 inside 10 yd of the ball plays off, at least 3.5 yd deep. A nickel or dime back stands just inside #2 at 5.5 yd, or over his man. The front is Even, Over, Under or Wide 9 (Wide 9 twice as often on 3rd and long); the goal line plays its own gap front.
  - *Red zone.* Inside the 20 the shells compress (no safety starts deeper than 4 yd plus half the distance to the goal line), the press share rises by 0.2 and Cover 1 brackets the inside (see Coverage).
- **Coverage families.** The ten calls:
  - **Cover 3**: three deep, four under. The thirds split 12 yd either side of the ball, so the middle owns the seams. The rotation is sky (the strong safety to the curl/flat), buzz (the strong safety to the hook) or cloud (a corner squats in the flat and the safety takes his third).
  - **Cover 1**: man across and the free safety deep at 17 yd; the sixth man robs, rushes or spies (60, 30 and 10 % of calls before the situation weighs in).
  - **Cover 2**: two deep halves, the corners in the flats at 5 yd, the hooks walling #2's seam and the Mike in the hole at 12 yd.
  - **Tampa 2**: Cover 2 with the Mike running the deep middle.
  - **Cover 4**: quarters, with three under.
  - **Cover 6**: quarters to the strength, Cover 2 to the weak side.
  - **2-man**: two deep halves and five men under in man.
  - **Cover 0**: six rush and five play man with no help, in a double A-gap, linebacker-and-safety, double-edge or corner blitz (the corner only when the weak #1 is within 14 yd of the ball).
  - **Fire zone**: five rush while an end drops to the seam or a tackle to the hook, 6 yd deep; three under, three deep.
  - **Sim pressure**: four rush and seven cover. A lineman drops 6 yd and a linebacker or safety rushes in his place, on 55 % of calls on a 0.3–0.8 s delay (0.35 s at most from 4 yd off or deeper).
- **Coverage.** Until he flips, a coverage man faces the quarterback: he backpedals at 65 % of top speed and opens up after 7 yd, or when a vertical comes within 3 yd of him; the flip halves his grip for 0.25 s. A deep zone man bails in a crossover run instead. A flipped play draws the mirror image of the same coverage.
  - *Zones.* Zones pattern-match. A deep man settles at 17 yd in an outside third, 18 yd in a half and 20 yd in the deep middle, and takes every vertical in his zone: on top of one, between two, leaning to the one nobody else carries. An underneath man takes one receiver, sits 0.5 yd off him in the throwing lane, and carries a vertical until a deep man is 1–8 yd over him; with no deep man he gives it up at 16 yd at Rookie and 21 yd at Pro, and keeps it at All-pro. A man with nobody to match leans toward the most open receiver near him.
  - *Man.* Press: a man corner mirrors at the line for 0.45 s after his get-off and jams once, then trails (or stays on top with no help). Trail, with help over the top: a step (0.5 yd) behind on a vertical and 0.6 yd to his leverage side, in the throwing lane on anything else. Off: he lines up 6.5 yd deep and keeps a 4 yd cushion at the line, giving up 0.7 yd of it for each yard the receiver gains, never closer than 1.2 yd. On top, with no help (Cover 0, or a Cover 1 receiver wider than 9 yd from the free safety): 1.2 yd over him. Beaten deep, he sits in the hip pocket, 0.8 yd off toward the quarterback. Leverage is outside on 65 % of Cover 1 calls (the free safety helps inside) and inside on 80 % of 2-man and 70 % of Cover 0 calls.
  - *Zone corners.* A zone corner who shows press lines up on #1 and bails to his zone at the snap; an off corner gives up the quick throw. Each family has its own press share (25 % in Cover 3, 45 % in Cover 2, 70 % in the fire zone and sim pressure), higher in the red zone and against a quick game.
  - *Banjo.* Against a bunch the men on it banjo: 0.55 s after the snap, plus their read time, they settle who takes whom, outside to outside, by where the receivers will be 0.5 s later.
  - *Quarters.* The corner carries #1 once he is past 5 yd going upfield at over 5 yd/s, or anything vertical past 8 yd; otherwise he squats 2.2 yd over him, no shallower than 7 yd, and 1.2 yd over a hitch. The safety reads #2: short and coming, he sits 5 yd over him, no shallower than 9 yd; vertical, he carries him; out or under, he robs the window inside #1 between 6 and 18 yd, 1.5 yd deeper than the receiver, and caps #1's post or dig however deep it goes. A trips #3 going vertical inside #1 is his if nobody has him.
  - *Cover 2 and Tampa 2.* The hooks wall #2's seam and carry it to the half; a release to the flat is the corner's. A half widens with #1 going vertical, 0.6 of the way, unless a seam inside him runs as fast. The Tampa 2 Mike runs the deep middle to 22 yd: he takes verticals within 9 yd of the ball and carries them as far as they go, and leaves anything short of 8 yd that is not straight upfield to the hooks.
  - *Robber and spy.* A robber sits in the hole (9 yd in Cover 1, 8 yd in Cover 0), jumps crossers within 10 yd of the ball and hands a vertical off at 12 yd; with nobody to take, he follows the quarterback's throwing motion (half of it at Pro, all of it at All-pro, not at Rookie). A free underneath zone defender, one with nobody to match, has zone eyes too: once he sees the wind-up (his reaction time after it starts) he breaks to the line the quarterback's shoulders point down, at his own depth, up to 6 yd from where he was playing (half the way at Pro, all the way at All-pro; a Rookie holds his landmark until he finds the ball). A deep middle splitting two verticals leans toward the one the quarterback turns to. A spy (in Cover 1, Cover 3 and sim pressure) mirrors the quarterback 4.5 yd past the line at 90 % speed and attacks once he leaves the pocket. A man whose receiver stays in to block robs the shallow middle at 7 yd and helps on the most open man near him.
  - *Plaster.* Once the quarterback scrambles, or holds the ball past 3.5 s, every zone defender takes the nearest uncovered receiver man to man (keeping him unless another is 2 yd nearer) and the man defenders stay glued. Nobody but a spy leaves coverage for a quarterback who can still throw, with one exception: when he runs at the line (seen running upfield at 2.5 yd/s or more, out of the pocket or within 3 yd of the line, and not winding up), the nearest underneath zone defender or robber within 14 yd comes up for him, from 6 yd behind the line at Pro and 10 yd at All-pro. The man is picked once and has committed, so a throw over his head finds his zone empty. A Rookie defense waits until the quarterback is over the line.
  - *Run reads and play action.* On a run each man reads it at his own time, then pursues the back, before the handoff too: linebackers 0.75 s after the snap at Rookie, 0.6 s at Pro and 0.45 s at All-pro, safeties 0.15 s later and corners 0.3 s later, up to 0.12 s sooner against a running offense. On a play fake a linebacker steps downhill at 40 % speed (until 0.83, 0.70 or 0.58 s by level, longer against a running offense and shorter against a passing one), and a safety within 11 yd of the line bites half as much. He bites on the fake he sees: once the quarterback stops selling it, he drops after his reaction time.
  - *Breaking on the ball.* A man defender sees the ball within about 110° of where he faces; a zone defender, eyes on the quarterback, sees it over either shoulder. Once he has picked it up, within 4 yd of the receiver under it he stays in phase, 0.45 yd to the side the ball comes in on, and plays it through his hands. Otherwise he goes to the earliest point where the ball will be catchable (0.9–2.3 yd up) that he can reach first after a 0.2 s turn; with none in reach he runs through the catch point (within 15 yd) or rallies to the catch (within 30 yd).
  - *Red zone.* Inside the 20 the end line is the deep help: a deep man plays a vertical in phase, 1 yd over him, instead of keeping a cushion. Cover 1 brackets the inside: the free safety takes only verticals within 9 yd of the ball, and a wide receiver's man plays him alone, on top. Within 5 yd of the goal line an underneath or man defender sits 0.7 yd on the goal-line side of his receiver.
- **Pass protection.** The line protects a spot, by rules: it does not know who comes. The rush's timings by level are in [Difficulty](#difficulty).
  - *The launch point.* The rush aims at, and the line protects, a launch point: at the quarterback's drop depth (deeper if he drops deeper) until he sets, then where he set, within 2 yd of the centre and 4.5–10 yd deep. He has set once he slows under 1 yd/s after 0.45 s, or at 1.2 s whatever he does (1.2 s after the fake on play action). The pocket box reaches 4.4 yd either side of it. When the quarterback climbs or slides inside it, nobody on the line moves with him: he slides off the blocks instead of pushing them.
  - *Sets.* Tackles kick-slide 0.9 yd out and 2 yd back; guards and the centre set 1.1–1.2 yd back. Each blocker meets his rusher in the lane, 0.95 yd in front of him toward the launch point, no more than 2.2–2.4 yd from his set.
  - *The count.* At the snap the line counts every lineman, anyone walked up within 2.5 yd of the line and 8 yd of the ball, anyone in the box (6 yd deep, 5.5 yd wide) and any blitzer who gave it away, and slides toward the side with more (on a tie, away from the back). It sees a man come 0.3 s after he goes, and sees a counted man drop once he is 1 yd deeper (2 yd to the side) or backing off at 2 yd/s.
  - *The rules.* Each lineman works a zone 2.5 yd either side of his set, the centre and the slide side shifted 1.6 yd toward the slide. The line blocks whoever would reach the quarterback first, so the man left free is the farthest; a counted man who has not shown is a maybe, and nobody leaves a rusher for him. A lineman keeps a man he is sure of until the man drops, leaves his zone or gets past him, so a man the count missed, or one who shows where a committed man was, can come free.
  - *The back.* He checks for a free rusher for 1.2 s (unless he has a hot route) and blocks only a man he has seen come; the line goes first. Once he has blocked he stays in, beaten or not; with nobody by 1.2 s he releases.
  - *Locks.* A blocker within 1.3 yd of a rusher, and nearer the quarterback, locks up with him; a pair more than 2.2 yd apart lets go. Locked, the rusher drives his blocker back along his own lane, bent at most 0.6 yd toward where he has seen the quarterback go: 0.75 yd/s for an end on a tackle, 1.1 for a tackle on a guard, 1.2 for a nose on the centre, 0.6 for a blitzer on a lineman and 1.6 against a back.
  - *Contests.* A locked rusher wins at a rate of win × (t / 1.4 s)^2.5 per second, t the time since the snap, so pressure builds while the quarterback holds the ball. At Pro, win is 0.77 for an end, 0.53 for a tackle, 0.48 for a nose, 0.86 for a blitzer and 1.9 against a back, the same at every level. What the level changes is the plan: each rep the blocker sets for one move and so gives up another (oversetting for the speed rush gives up the counter inside, a short set for the counter gives up the bull, anchoring for the bull gives up speed). A Pro rusher runs his move against a set nobody has read, at the matchup's rate. At All-pro 80 % of rushers read the set and run the move it gives, at four times the rate, and 80 % have a counter ready: one whose first move has stalled 0.5 s switches to it. At Rookie 75 % of rushers give their move away with the same stance every rep, and the blocker sets for it: a quarter of the rate. A free lineman within 1.6 yd halves it. A rusher closing within 3.5 yd of the quarterback adds up to 3 a second (in full at 1.5 yd), falling to a quarter of that as the quarterback runs from 2 to 5 yd/s. A rusher ridden level with a quarterback who stepped up, and wide of him, keeps 35 % of his rate. Each rep's rush is between a sixth as strong and twice as strong, and each rusher's move 0.66 to 1.5 times on top, so some reps get home fast and others slowly.
  - *Moves.* Each rusher draws a move when he locks up. A speed rush runs the arc at up to 4.5 yd/s, the tackle sliding with him, and wins outside; a bull rush drives the blocker straight back at 1.7 times the push, so the pocket shrinks without a win; a counter (spin or swim) wins inside. Ends speed rush half the time, tackles bull rush 45 % of the time. Ends lined up wider than 3.5 yd rush the arc, 1.5–3 yd outside the launch point and up to 0.8 yd past it, and turning the corner takes a win; inside men push their gap (the A, B and C gaps at 0.8, 2.4 and 4.2 yd from the centre).
  - *Twists.* An end and the tackle beside him twist on 8 % of pass reps at Rookie, 15 % at Pro and 28 % at All-pro: the tackle first (T-E) or the end first (E-T), never on a pressure with more than five rushers. The line sees 75 % of them and passes the penetrator to the next man; otherwise the looper comes free until someone inside picks him up.
  - *The win.* A winner rips past at 6.2 yd/s to the side he won and leans that way for 0.5 s. Nobody can pick him up for 0.5 s, and the beaten blocker stumbles for 0.5 s. How far he turns at the quarterback, rather than running on up his arc, is the difficulty's bend: 1 (straight at him) at All-pro, 0.7 at Pro, 0.5 at Rookie. A spare blocker, or the beaten man once he is back in front, can pick him up again.
  - *Recognition and sheds.* A rusher sees the quarterback leave the pocket after his reaction time plus 0.2 s (Rookie), 0.12 s (Pro) or 0.06 s (All-pro) and up to 0.1 s of his own. A break for the edge (within 0.75 yd of the box edge, running out at 3 yd/s or more) he reads 0.35 s ahead; a slide inside the pocket draws no reaction. A locked rusher then fights to get off his block: an attempt of 0.3–0.6 s, crossing at 1.4 yd/s, that works at the matchup's shed chance (0.85 for an end, 0.6 for a tackle, 0.55 for a nose, 0.8 for a blitzer, 0.9 on a back), the same at every level, and only 35 % as often when his blocker is square between him and the quarterback. A failed attempt can go again after 0.2 s; a good one bursts off at 4.5 yd/s, and that blocker cannot take him again for 1 s.
  - *Contain and chase.* The widest edge rusher on the side the quarterback breaks to keeps contain as soon as he sees the break, before the quarterback is out. He aims 0.8, 1.5 or 2 yd (by level) outside the point where he can meet the quarterback's path, 1–3 yd in front of him, and runs outside at 80 % of top speed even while blocked (his blocker is on his inside). Within 2.5 yd, level or in front, he takes him on. The others chase: the backside from behind, inside men 0.8 yd inside-out so they do not overrun him. Linemen keep riding their men; one with nobody drifts toward the quarterback at 40 % speed, no more than 2.5 yd from his set, and a new block holds only a rusher who has not yet seen the quarterback go, from in his path.
  - *Blitzers.* A second-level blitzer times the count: he goes 0.2 s after the snap at Pro and 0.12 s at All-pro instead of reacting at 0.28 s. At Rookie he goes at 0.28 s like everyone else, and 80 % of Rookie blitzers give it away before the snap, so the line counts them. Blitzers rush flat out, linemen at 92 %. A free rusher takes the straight lane to the launch point, 1.2 yd around blocks in his way (outside them for an edge rusher).
  - *Drop-outs.* A man who stops rushing (a lineman dropping into coverage) is let go and counted as dropped. A delayed rusher shows a 1.5 yd drop at half speed until his count, then comes like any blitzer.
  - *Play action.* Until he reads pass, a defensive lineman fits his gap 0.3 yd off the line at half speed and, blocked, holds his ground; his pressure clock starts when he reads pass. He reads it his reaction time after the quarterback stops selling the fake, and no later than 0.9 s (Rookie), 0.75 s (Pro) or 0.6 s (All-pro) after the snap. A called blitzer comes anyway.
  - *After the throw.* A rusher who has seen the ball go (his reaction time after the release) comes off his block and runs to where it comes down, carried on along the receiver he sees under it for up to 1.5 s.
  - *After a catch or a scramble.* Linemen hold their men until each defender's shed time (2.5–5.3 s after the snap), and a blocked defender sheds 0.3 s after the runner comes within 1.6 yd. The receivers block downfield (see *Free blockers* under Run blocking).
- **Run blocking** (`dist/js/sim/run.js`). Every run block is a matchup with its own life.
  - *The plan.* On inside and outside zone each lineman takes the man over him or in his play-side gap, play side first; a lineman with nobody there doubles his neighbour's man and climbs to the nearest linebacker once that linebacker moves (he has read the run and runs at 2 yd/s or more), comes within 2.6 yd of the double team or 1.2 yd of the line, or 0.8 s into the block. On power and counter the linemen on the play side of the puller block down, the back side hinges to cover the puller's gap, and the puller kicks out the first man he finds in the hole or leads up on a linebacker. A lineman climbing to a linebacker runs at the point where he can cut that linebacker off on his way to the ball, not at where he stands. Nobody blocks a man behind the runner.
  - *The pull.* The pulling guard opens at the snap (a 0.18 s pull step), runs flat 1.3 yd behind the line at full speed and turns up 1.6 yd short of the called gap, about 1.5 s after the snap on power and counter. He looks for work the whole way: a free defender within 3 yd in his path, shallower than 2 yd past the line, ends the pull there. His man is the free defender who would be in the hole first, no deeper than 6 yd, the men on the line counted 0.35 s sooner (the kick-out before the linebacker).
  - *Free blockers* (`dist/js/sim/support.js`). A blocker with nobody takes the most dangerous man: the free defender who would meet the ball carrier first, running flat out at the carrier's present run (before the handoff, at the hole the play is called to). That goes for the receivers on a run, after a catch and for a quarterback over the line, and for a lineman on a run whose own man is gone: shed, down, behind the runner or, from 0.9 s, out of reach (no longer a threat within 4 s, or past the point where the lineman could still get in his way). The threats are handed out most dangerous first, each to the blocker who can be in his way soonest, at the first point of the defender's line to the runner the blocker reaches no more than 0.1 s after him (at 85 % of his speed); a man he cannot cut off is not his, and a blocker keeps his man unless another beats him to that man by 0.35 s. A defender who is not playing the ball yet (farther than 5 yd from it and not running at it at 2 yd/s or more: a corner still backing off in coverage) is no threat: the receiver runs him off instead of stopping to block him. Nobody takes a man more than 1 yd behind the runner. A blocker with nobody to block gets 5 yd in front of the ball and 2.5 yd to his own side of it (on a run, once the back is 1 yd past the line); one behind the play follows it, and a lineman with nobody trails the runner downfield. With a pass in the air to somebody else the other receivers see it 0.25 s after the release and go to the man they will block, leaving alone anybody who will be at the catch within 0.3 s of the ball, and wait 2 yd off him: nobody is touched before the catch. All of it is the same at every difficulty, from where the defenders are now, never from where they will cut.
  - *The margin.* Each block has a margin in doublings of power: the blockers' mass^2.5 against the defender's (a lineman on a linebacker starts about 0.75 up, on a defensive lineman about even), each man's form on the rep (2^±1, from the draw every player makes at the snap), the line's day (±0.6 on every block of the rep), the snap count (+0.55 for the blockers, who know where the play goes), a double team (the second man adds 85 % of his power), an angle (+0.35 for a down block or the puller's kick-out), a reach on a man shaded to the play side (−0.35), the defender's read of a down block, a reach or a puller (by level, below), and a linebacker's or defensive back's read of a block in space: one thrown by free blockers only (a lineman off his own man, a receiver), which he sees coming (nothing at Rookie, 0.4 to him at Pro, 0.9 at All-pro).
  - *The life.* Without the ball near, a block holds about 2.3 × 2^(0.8 m) s (0.35–8 s), coming apart on a rising hazard with a threshold drawn for the block, so some blocks fall off early and some last the play. The runner pulls a defender off it: within 2.4 yd he adds to the hazard, more for a runner coming straight at him and most when the blocker is not between them, and a runner who runs into him (within 0.75 yd) is his. So the man the back runs at sheds first, a man whose blocker has lost the angle comes off at once, and a sealed man the back runs past stays sealed. A blocker who wins by more than 0.5 drives his man back at up to 1.8 yd/s for 2.5 yd; a defender who wins by more than 0.5 walks his blocker into the backfield at up to 2 yd/s, and when he sheds a blocker he beat by 0.5 stumbles; on a quarter of blocks won by more than 1.3 the defender is knocked down for 0.9 s. A receiver's stalk block holds 60 % as long as a lineman's, and a free lineman's block on the move 75 %. A blocker does not take back a man who got off him for 1 s.
  - *What it looks like.* Some reps the lane opens wide and some it closes at once. The scripted carrier (bench, 1050 designed runs a level, random calls) gains about 6.1 yd a carry at Rookie, 4.5 at Pro and 4.1 at All-pro; at All-pro about 7 % of carries lose yards, 47 % gain 0–2, 9 % gain 8 or more and 4 % gain 15 or more, and every play has its stuffs and its long runs.
- **Run fits.** Once a defender reads the run he fits it. The gaps (A beside the centre, B between guard and tackle, C outside the tackle or between him and a tight end, D outside that) move with the line as a whole. Defensive linemen own the gap they line up in, linebackers and box safeties take the nearest gap left, and a deep safety fills a gap still open on some reps (by level) and then reads the run with the linebackers; one who does not stays deep 0.25 s longer. A man pursues the runner on his gap's side of him, so every gap stays owned as the play moves; a back-side man who stays home keeps 2 yd on the cutback side, and one who does not chases the play 1.5 yd past the runner and leaves the cutback. A bust jumps the next gap toward the play, over-runs the play by 2.5 yd, or reads the run 0.35 s late. Once the runner is 3 yd through the line, or the man is behind him, it is open-field pursuit.
- **Playing the ball.** A defender must locate the pass, face its arrival and reach it at a catchable height before attempting a play. Clean catches, swats and misses resolve separately, with one attempt per defender per pass. An engaged blocker cannot intercept through a block. Higher difficulty improves recognition, route anticipation and flight estimates; player speed, reach and catch odds stay the same.
- **Tackling.** In the open field a pursuer takes an angle: he aims at the first point of the carrier's path, as he saw it, that he can reach in time, looking up to 2.5 s ahead, so a well-timed cutback makes him overrun. That path bends along the sideline 25.8 yd from the middle: the sideline fences the carrier in. A pursuer who cannot get there chases 0.6 s ahead of the carrier and, when the carrier is more than 6 yd from the middle and the pursuer is inside him, stays 1.2 yd inside him, leaving him only the sideline. Each pursuer shades 1–1.5 yd to his own side of the path, fading out from 7 to 2.5 yd away, so they arrive side by side. A free defender within reach gets one tackle attempt, but not through a blocker: an offensive player within 0.3 yd of the line between them takes it away. A man who gets his hands on a runner who has not just cut usually brings him down: about 85 % head on, a little less from the side or from behind when he is slower than the runner. A cut or juke in the last 0.3 s, or a cut that leaves a man who came in hot running past, drops that to about 50 %, so a well-timed move still breaks free; a tackler under control (slower than 72 % of his top speed) rides out 45 % of the move and still makes it about two times in three. Tackling odds are the same at every difficulty; what a level decides is who comes to balance: a pursuer in front of the runner (the runner heading within 60° of him at 3 yd/s or more), within 0.22 s of meeting him at the rate they close and no more than 3.5 yd away, shortens his stride to 68 % of his top speed and squares up to where the runner is, then strikes inside 1.1 yd. One in five defenders does it at Rookie, three in five at Pro and nine in ten at All-pro; a man chasing from behind or from the side never does. When two men reach the runner on the same step and the first misses, the second hits a runner already off balance and makes it more often. A tackle wraps the runner and drags him down within a second, giving 0–3 real yards after contact (about one yard typically), and other defenders who reach him join the wrap; a miss leaves the tackler stumbling for about half a second and costs the carrier a third of his speed. Pursuers who cannot close the last yard dive, at lower odds the farther they dive. On runs, after a catch and for a quarterback over the line the receivers stalk-block the most dangerous man each can cut off (*Free blockers* under Run blocking): a receiver locks on within 1.2 yd; after a catch he holds his man for at most 1.3 s before letting him go for good, however late in the down, and the runner coming within 1.6 yd of a blocked man pulls him off the block 0.3 s later; on a designed run the block lives like any run block (a receiver holds 60 % as long as a lineman). Passes can be dropped, more often when contested or when a bullet is thrown from close range. Sacks, interceptions, out of bounds, touchdowns and safeties end the rep.
- **Practice tools.** Forty calls across shotgun doubles, spread, trips, trey, bunch, empty, tight, pistol and singleback alignments, each of which can be flipped. The book has the quick game (Slants, Double Slant, Stick, Spread Stick, Snag, Spot, Spacing, Ohio), intermediate concepts (Mesh, Levels, Tight End Cross, Drive Shallow, Doubles Drive, Dagger, Smash, Curl-flat, Hank, Sail), shots (Four Verticals, Mills, Scissors, Post-wheel, Dig-wheel, Switch Verticals, Sluggo, Post-corner, Deep Over), red-zone calls (Fade-smash, Shallow Mesh), four play-action calls and seven runs. Power, Counter and the Trey weak sweep use pulling guards; zone and stretch use different blocking tracks. Filter by formation or run/pass, search by concept or read, and review the route diagram and coaching cue; the Playbook marks the offensive coordinator's likeliest calls for the down as *Suggested*. The cue names each receiver by his pass key (A, W, S, D or Space; a jersey number on touch screens) in a badge of his route colour, the same label the throw buttons and helmet badges show. New sessions start in **Move the chains** on **All-pro**, with the coordinators calling offense (**Coordinator's call**) and defense each down; choose a play to repeat it. Pre-snap hot routes (streak, slant, drag, in, out, curl, flat, corner) for any of the five receivers and audibles to any play. The **Defense** setting takes the coordinator's call (**Random every rep**) or names one of the ten families, which still disguises its shell; on a random call the score bug reads *Disguised* until the snap, then names the package and the family (*Nickel · Cover 3*). Five personnel packages, four fronts and a goal-line gap front, and three difficulty levels (see [Difficulty](#difficulty)). Repeat-spot reps, or **Move the chains** with downs, distance and a first-down line. Instant half-speed replay with scrubbing, and session totals on the pause screen, including the NFL passer rating to one decimal (throw-aways count as attempts; a dash until the first attempt).

### Difficulty

Difficulty is how smart the defense is, not how fast or strong it is. Bodies, reach, catching, tackling and the strength of every block are the same at every level, and so is everything about the offense: speed, acceleration, the jog without sprint (80 % of top speed), stamina (a full tank lasts 9 s of sprinting), the carrier's moment after a catch and the tackling odds. What changes is the defense's decisions: the coordinator's calls, disguises and scouting, the rush plan, run fits and gap discipline, how a lineman reads a down block or a reach and a linebacker a block in space, who comes to balance before a tackle, who comes up for a quarterback who runs, and how well coverage players read routes, the quarterback and the ball. The ten teammates (routes, protection, the pull, whom a free blocker takes) play the same at every level. A ball carrier on a designed run is read almost as late at every level (0.32, 0.28 and 0.26 s); the All-pro step on the ground comes from fits, reads and the rush plan.

## Front end

The title screen sits over a slow cinematic pass across the facility: the FOOTBALLSIM wordmark, a vertical **Practice / Playbook / Settings / Controls** menu (keyboard, mouse, touch or controller), and a thin bar with the graphics tier, frame rate and input device.

In play, a broadcast-style score bug shows field position and rep (or down, distance and yards to go when moving the chains). Badges with each receiver's pass key (his jersey number on touch screens) ride above the receivers' helmets in their route colours, a pass meter appears beside the quarterback while a throw charges, the play call sits in a small card at the lower left, and each result slides in as a lower-third banner. An optional field strip (Settings, off by default) shows the whole field with the line of scrimmage, the line to gain and every player. Playbook, Hot routes, Audible, Settings, Controls and Paused share one dialog style; Pause can return to the title menu.

Interface type is Barlow Condensed and Barlow (OFL, vendored), with a system-font fallback.

## Adaptive graphics

Auto starts at Balanced, waits through a warm-up, and lowers quality after repeated slow two-second windows; sustained headroom raises it again. Changes apply between reps; a manual choice stays fixed. Hidden tabs stop rendering and pause the game, dialogs suspend the simulation and audio, the title screen renders at 20 fps and play is capped at 60 fps.

| Profile | Pixel budget | Max pixel ratio | Sun shadow map | Environment detail | Grass tufts | Small details |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Performance | 800,000 | 0.85 | none (contact shadows only) | 35 % of the trees with alpha-tested leaf cards, no small sideline props, simple turf shading, athletes beyond 20 yd at reduced detail | 0 | off |
| Balanced | 1,500,000 | 1.15 | 1024² | 70 % of the trees with alpha-tested leaf cards, full sideline, detailed turf without anisotropic filtering, athletes beyond 40 yd at reduced detail | 9,000 | on |
| High | 2,500,000 | 1.5 | 2048² | all trees with alpha-to-coverage leaf cards, full sideline, detailed turf with 8x anisotropic filtering, full-detail athletes | 22,000 | on |
| Ultra | 4,200,000 | 2 | 4096² | as High | 45,000 | on |

Post-processing (`dist/js/env/post.js`) is tied to the same profiles; each tier adds passes deliberately:

| Profile | Scene target | Bloom | Ambient occlusion | Depth of field (title, replay) | Night shadows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Performance | the canvas, directly (renderer tone mapping, no post passes) | none | none | none | none |
| Balanced | HDR, 4x multisampled; one composite pass (tone mapping, grade, vignette, grain) | 4-level pyramid from half resolution | none | none | nearest pole bank |
| High | as Balanced | 5 levels | 8 samples per pixel at half resolution, depth-aware blur | on | two nearest pole banks |
| Ultra | as Balanced | 6 levels | 12 samples | on | two nearest pole banks |

Without float render targets the game falls back to drawing straight to the canvas on every profile.

"Small details" are the goal-post wind ribbons and the athletes' small parts (towels, tape, belts, chin straps). Reduced-detail athletes (about 2,800 instead of 11,000 triangles, no small details) are drawn from a second set of meshes on the same skeletons, chosen per athlete by distance from the camera. Rendering quality never changes the 120 Hz simulation. At the lowest tier Auto can also lower the resolution. These are budgets, not promised frame rates. WebGL 2 is required.

## Scope and realism

This is an original browser game inspired by the practice modes of modern football games. It is candid about what it is:

- **Everything visual is procedural.** The athletes are built from lofted superellipse tubes and simple primitives and posed by hand-written procedural animation and inverse kinematics; there are no scanned players, motion capture, motion matching or ragdolls. The facility is modelled from simple shapes and generated textures. Grass detail comes from CC0 photographs and the three.js lawn texture, re-tinted by the field shader; the field markings are painted analytically.
- **The physics is a model, not an engine.** Players are points with momentum, grip and a facing; bodies do not collide except through simple separation, blocking engagement and tackle rules. Tackles, catches, drops, pass-rush wins and block sheds are resolved with probabilities on top of that model. The numbers are tuned to plausible NFL figures (about 1.8 s ten-yard splits and 5.1 s forty-yard times for a back) but are not measured from real players.
- **Football is simplified.** Offense only, nine alignments using the same 11 personnel (one back, one tight end, three receivers), 40 calls, against five defensive personnel packages (base 4-3, nickel 4-2-5, dime 4-1-6, heavy 4-4-3, a 5-3-3 goal line) from a squad of fifteen defenders, with ten coverage families and four fronts. The offense has no substitutions and no pre-snap motion. No fumbles, penalties, intentional grounding, defensive control, special teams, clock or complete NFL rules. Throw-away simply ends the rep incomplete.
- **No licensed content.** No NFL teams, league marks, players, commentary or Madden assets.

## Development

Requires Node 22 or later. The runtime and the core tests have no package dependencies. The first run needs network access to download the pinned third-party files; later runs use the verified local copies.

```sh
npm run dev       # vendor assets, then http://localhost:8000
npm test          # deterministic simulation and graphics tests
npm run check     # syntax and local imports of every module, vendored assets
npm run build     # standalone .pages-dist release
```

Browser checks (a development-only dependency):

```sh
npm install --no-save --package-lock=false --ignore-scripts playwright@1.56.1
npx playwright install chromium
node scripts/serve.mjs .pages-dist
# In a second terminal:
node tests/browser.mjs
```

The browser test drives the real UI: title menu, camera fallback and cycling, play search, formation filters, formation alignment, hot routes, pass keys and their labels (flipped too), passing, handoff, replay, pause and the passer rating, settings and the performance tier, Move the chains, paged audibles, fullscreen, the 390 px phone layout and the return to the menu. It fails on any console error or failed request, and its screenshots are uploaded by the verification workflow. Add `?debug=1` for a developer-only `window.footballsim` handle (`sim`, `view`, `graphics`, `start()`, `snap()`).

Code layout: `dist/js/sim.js` with `sim/` (simulation: the offensive and defensive coordinators, coverage, protection) and `plays.js` (playbook), `scene.js` with `environment.js` and `env/` (field, facility, sky, lighting, cameras, grass), `athletes.js` with `athletes/` (rigs, skinning, poses, ball), `characters/` (the optional skinned-model layer, see `MODELS.md`), `app.js`, `audio.js`, `index.html` and `style.css` (front end), and `graphics.js` (quality tiers).

All game-specific code and procedural assets were created for this repository. Third-party credits: `ASSETS.md`.

### Benchmark

`scripts/bench.mjs` plays the real simulation with a scripted quarterback and ball carrier, so changes to the defense are measured, not felt. By difficulty and coverage it reports completion %, yards per attempt and per play, sack, interception, breakup and drop rates, pressure, time to throw and explosive plays. Every rep is seeded, so two versions of the game compare on the same reps.

```sh
node scripts/bench.mjs --reps 200                  # every difficulty x every coverage
node scripts/bench.mjs --mode drive --reps 50      # move the chains: points per drive
node scripts/bench.mjs --watch 7 mesh cover3       # one rep, traced every 0.25 s
node scripts/bench.mjs --sim /tmp/base/dist/js     # another copy of dist/js, e.g. from git archive
```

The quarterback sees only what the screen shows and knows the called play. From 1.0 s (1.3 s on play action) he scores each receiver's openness every 0.1 s, gives the first read 0.4 s, then throws to the best man open enough for his aggression. He checks down after 2.6 s, escapes or throws the ball away under pressure, and never holds it past 4.5 s. `--help` lists every option and column; `--json` writes every rep.
